# Shot planning

## Inputs

Narration, evidence ledger, format, pace limits, and available assets or visual research leads.

## Workflow

1. Divide sequences where information, viewpoint or emotional distance changes. Assign a duration and narration or intentional silence to each shot.
2. Specify shot size, angle, subject, focal point, motion, lighting, transition, and sound. Change scale or viewpoint between adjacent shots when it clarifies the story.
3. Use readable overhead maps, timelines, diagrams or scale comparisons when spatial or numerical facts carry the scene. Plan caption-safe framing and start/end poses.
4. Match moves to evidence. Document crop and depth assumptions for archive assets. Plan a feasible reconstruction for gaps and mark it as such.
5. Hand visual requirements to visual-asset-researcher, layout to visual-director, and deterministic movement to motion-designer.

## Outputs and checks

Deliver a machine-readable shot list with timing that sums to the cut. Check frame-level cuts, label readability, viewpoint variety, focal priority, and alignment with narration. Reserve titles for earned beats.
