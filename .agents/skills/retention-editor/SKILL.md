# Retention editing

## Inputs

Draft script, scene or shot durations, target audience, measured speech if available, and a preview for second-pass review.

## Workflow

1. Check the first sentences for a concrete event, consequence and question. Check the ending for an answer and an image or decision the viewer can remember.
2. Mark the first new piece of evidence in each sequence. Remove repeated facts and move background next to the decision it explains.
3. Audit consecutive shots for repeated framing, motion and information. Alternate scale, sources, maps and details when the subject warrants it.
4. Locate intentional pauses and use them around reversals, impact and aftermath. Check captions and spoken pacing after rendering.
5. Return changes to storytelling-director and cinematic-shot-planner, then hand an approved beat map to scene-generator and video-quality-review.

## Outputs and checks

Deliver a beat map, revision notes and preview timecodes. Each major question should pay off. Flag slow passages and visual repetition with timecodes and evidence.
